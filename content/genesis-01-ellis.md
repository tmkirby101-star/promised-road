# Chapter 1 — A World Made Good
### Genesis 1:1–2:3 · Ellis (Grade 1) · *The Promised Road*
#### Build specification (consolidated · single source of truth)

> Working proposals, not locked forever but settled for this build: **translation = WEB** (quotations
> shown visually distinct from our retelling); **guide = Ezra, provisional** (the guide *reads and
> points to* Scripture and is **never** presented as an eyewitness to events he didn't see);
> **memory verse = Genesis 1:1** this chapter (Genesis 1:27 is taught in the next chapter on
> humanity's calling); **ordering activity = four creation moments (Days 1–4)**, while the **story
> itself tells all six days and the seventh‑day rest**.

---

## 1. The big idea — what this chapter reveals about God

> **God made everything, and it was very good.**

Stated plainly for Ellis — **"God looked at everything He had made. It was very good."** Creation is
good because God made it good and truly declared it so; **not** because it is useful to us, and not as
an arbitrary label. What Ellis comes to see about *God*:

- **God is the Maker** — "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth" (1:1).
- **God is powerful and good** — He speaks, and it is; He calls it good, then very good (1:3, 1:31).
- **God is generous** — He made a good world and gave people a place and a job in it (1:28).

The Fracture and the Promise (later stops) are not introduced here.

---

## 2. The learning model (this determines what we store)

### One authoritative record, two kinds of signal
Each explorer has **one** record. It holds two clearly separated kinds of signal:

1. **Understanding** — demonstrated by **activities** (the questions and the ordering task). This is
   what "discoveries" measure.
2. **Participation** — pages of the story **experienced**, Scripture practice **completed**, a
   response **written**, the application step **done**. Participation is **not** a claim of
   comprehension; it is simply "the child did this part."

Keeping these apart is deliberate: *reading the story* is participation, not proof of understanding.

### Understanding — evidence log + four honest states
Each understanding holds a **log of attempts**, each attempt recording: `correct` and
`usedAnswerHint`. State is derived as the **best** across attempts and is **sticky** — a later hinted
or wrong attempt is recorded on **that attempt only** and **never downgrades** an earlier independent
success (earned rank stays intact; parents can still see the later difficulty):

- **Not yet checked** — no attempts.
- **Practice needed** — attempted, not yet correct.
- **Completed with support** — correct, but with an **answer hint**.
- **Demonstrated independently** — correct with **no answer hint** (achieved once → it stays).

| Key | Understanding (child-facing) | Demonstrated by |
|----|------------------------------|-----------------|
| `creator` | **God made everything.** | Part 2 · Question 1 |
| `good` | **God called His creation good.** | Part 2 · Question 2 |
| `order` | **God made things in an order.** | Part 2 · ordering activity |
| `application` | **God gives people the job of caring for His world.** | Part 3 · care scenario |

Other activities (now or later) may add attempts to the same understanding, strengthening it or
revealing uncertainty.

### Access support ≠ answer support
- **Access supports** — narration (read‑aloud), enlarged text, accessible controls — are recorded
  **separately**, at the explorer level, and **never** affect independence. A child can *demonstrate
  independently while using narration.*
- **Answer support** — a hint or correction toward the answer — is what marks an attempt "with
  support."

### Discoveries vs. completion vs. rank
- **Discoveries** = how many of the four understandings are at least *completed with support* (0–4).
  Measures **understanding**.
- **Chapter completion** = a separate bar: **story experienced + Scripture practice completed +
  response saved (a meaningful attempt) + application done.** **Supported success counts; perfect
  recall or flawless writing never blocks completion.** Earns a **chapter completion marker** — not
  the Genesis stamp.
- **Rank** = a separate, **performance‑based, curriculum‑wide** system with **retakes**; preserved,
  not computed in this chapter, and never collapsed into discoveries.

### Writing
Ungraded in this prototype; children never see academic grades. The record stores the saved text and
that writing/revision happened. A **parent‑facing assessment against grade‑level writing standards —
with AI feedback and revision support — is planned**; the model leaves room for it.

## 3. Part 1 — The Telling  *(READ · participation)*

The whole chapter, grade‑1 voice. **Scripture quotations (WEB) are shown visually distinct** from our
retelling so a child never confuses the two. The guide reads and points to the text; he is **not**
portrayed as having witnessed creation.

> **"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."** — Genesis 1:1

- **Day 1 — Light.** It was dark. God *spoke:* "Let there be light!" — and there was light. God called
  the light **Day**, the dark **Night.** *("God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light." —
  Genesis 1:3)*
- **Day 2 — Sky.** God made the wide **sky** over the waters.
- **Day 3 — Land, Sea, Plants.** Dry **land** rose from the waters — **Earth** and **Seas** — and
  filled with grass, plants, and fruit trees. *("God saw that it was good." — Genesis 1:12)*
- **Day 4 — Sun, Moon, Stars.** God set the **sun** for the day, the **moon** and **stars** for the
  night, to mark days and seasons and years.
- **Day 5 — Fish and Birds.** The seas filled with swimming things; **birds** flew across the sky.
- **Day 6 — Animals and People.** God made the **animals**, and then **people**, in His own image.
  *("God created man in his own image… male and female he created them." — Genesis 1:27)* He
  **blessed** them and gave them a place and a job — to fill the earth and **care for** it. *(1:28)*

> **"God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."** — Genesis 1:31

- **Day 7 — Rest.** Everything was finished. God **rested** on the seventh day and made it holy.
  **God's rest means His work was complete — not that He was tired.** *(2:2–3)*

**Key words** (grade‑1): **Created** — God brought it into being; He made it so. **Very good** — just
the way God wanted it. **Care for** — to watch over something and help it, as God asked people to do.

*Reaching the end records the story pages as **experienced** (participation).*

## 4. Part 2 — Explore & Understand  *(activities + Scripture memory)*

**Question 1 — `creator`.** "Who made the world and everything in it?" → **God** ✓. Each wrong choice
points to the specific reason it can't be the Maker (the sun/sea were themselves *made*).

**Question 2 — `good`.** "When God looked at everything He had made, what did He say?" → **"It was
very good."** ✓. A wrong choice points back to what God *said* in Genesis 1:31.

*Each question records an attempt with `correct` and `usedAnswerHint` (independence tracked; access
supports never count against it).*

**Scripture memory (participation) — Genesis 1:1, supported practice.** Not cold recall:
- **Fill the blank:** "In the beginning, God ______ the heavens and the earth." (the full verse is
  shown first, then faded) — with optional narration.
Completing the practice records Scripture practice as **done** (participation; not an understanding
check, and recall need not be perfect).

**Ordering activity — `order` — "Put these four creation moments in order."** Days 1–4, tap‑to‑place
(drag optional later). Correct order: **Light → Sky → Land & Plants → Sun, Moon & Stars.**
- **Contextual help** (shows the task): "It was dark at first — what did God make **before** anything
  could be seen? Start there, then move forward."
- **Specific feedback** (the common miss): "Look again at **Light** and the **Sun.** Light came on
  **Day 1**, the sun on **Day 4.** Which came first?"
- First try correct → `order` **independent**; correct after a nudge → **with support** (and the
  earlier state is never downgraded by a later hint).

## 5. Part 3 — Respond & Write

**Prompt:** **"Choose something God made. Tell one thing you notice about it."**
**Example (an invitation, not a minimum):** *"God made birds. Birds have wings and feathers."* — two
sentences are modeled; a first grader's **meaningful attempt is honored**, and expectations strengthen
gradually across the curriculum.

- The chosen **picture stays pinned** through writing and review.
- **After writing, the app connects it back:** *"Birds are part of the creation God called very
  good."*
- **Gratitude is an optional invitation** ("If you like, add why you're thankful") — never required.

**Review is the child's action, not an app verdict** — the app asks, Ellis answers:
- "Did you name something God made?" → **Yes** / **Help me check**
- "Did you tell something you noticed?" → **Yes** / **Help me check**
The app never shows ✓ as if it verified the writing. Saving records the **response (participation)**.

**Application — `application` — its own short, care‑able scenario** (not forced onto the writing
subject): "God gives us the job of caring for His world. Here is a **plant** (or **pet**, or **messy
park**). Which is a good way to care for it?" Choosing a real way to care records an `application`
attempt (independent, or with a hint → with support). The child's writing subject stays whatever they
chose.

## 6. Completion & the passport

- The four understandings show as **learning progress** (not four auto‑badges).
- Finishing the chapter (participation bar in §2) earns a **chapter completion marker.**
- **Illustrated passport badges** are reserved for meaningful milestones and special discoveries.
- The **Genesis stamp** comes only after the **whole Genesis book and its review/finale.**

---

## 7. What the parent sees (reading the one record)

- **Understandings:** each as **Not yet checked / Practice needed / Completed with support /
  Demonstrated independently** — and where a child succeeded independently **then** later needed help,
  **both** are visible (the success isn't erased).
- **Participation:** story experienced · Scripture practice done · response saved · application done —
  shown **separately** from understanding.
- **Discoveries:** "X of 4." **Chapter:** in progress / complete (with marker).
- **Writing:** the child's actual saved text, "saved · [date]." Ungraded now; grade‑level assessment
  (AI + revision) is the planned next layer.
- **Supports used:** **access supports** (narration/large text) and **answer hints** shown
  separately, so evidence is read in context.
- **Rank:** surfaced from the separate curriculum‑wide performance system (with retakes).

---

## 8. Verification (an explicit build step)

One record reduces disagreement; it doesn't make it impossible. Before the chapter is called done we
test the full chain end‑to‑end — **activity result → evidence saved → parent display → progress** —
including a **failed save** and a **reload mid‑chapter** (which should offer *Continue where you left
off*).
