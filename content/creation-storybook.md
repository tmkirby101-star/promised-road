# Creation storybook review

Seven illustrated spreads at #book, from first light to seventh-day rest. This isolated preview preserves the working Ellis chapter and never writes chapter completion, learning evidence, or a Genesis stamp.

## Reading and framing
- Retelling and WEB Scripture appear separately. Isaiah speaks only in the learning transition, never as an eyewitness to Creation.
- Day One preserves the approved single cosmic radiance, with no stars, sun, water, or creatures. Days Two and Three have no celestial disks or creatures. Day Four introduces sun, moon, and stars; Day Five sea life and birds; Day Six land animals and people. Day Seven shows the finished world, not a tired God.
- Day Six includes image-bearing, human responsibility, and “very good.” Day Seven explains holy rest as completion.
- Two-page book on wider screens; fourteen individual faces on narrow screens or with bigger text. Overflow remains scrollable at large text sizes. Bookmark and text preference are browser-local and separate from educational records.
- Four selected moments are used for supported ordering practice. This is not an all-seven recall gate and awards no assessment evidence.

## Limits
Browser speech synthesis remains a device-dependent access support. Natural recorded narration, word highlighting, guide portraits, production auth, AI, and cloud persistence are separate tasks. This preview is not the completed curriculum or a complete Genesis book.

## Subject and paint direction
Each day has its own visual subject and composition. Day Three is a close botanical grove, Day Four contains only sun/moon/stars in the heavens, Day Six brings people and land animals into focus with broad traditional brushwork, and Day Seven shows the people and animals resting in a sheltered woodland clearing. The earlier coastal vista repetition and proposed globe for Day Seven are superseded. Scripture and narration remain unchanged; the rest scene is illustration, not a claim that Genesis narrates Adam and Eve's exact posture.
