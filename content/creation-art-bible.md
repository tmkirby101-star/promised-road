The owner approved this Creation illustration set on 2026-10-20. Future artwork — for
this chapter and the books that follow — should match this standard so the journey
stays visually coherent.

## Style
- Luminous, cinematic, painterly biblical storytelling. The soft traditional-oil look of
  Days 3 / 6 / 7 is the reference for new work. Welcoming to young children, substantial
  enough for older students.
- Each day has its OWN distinct subject and composition — no repeated coastal vistas.
- The paintings carry their natural range of color. Navy, gold, and cream are the
  interface, not the artwork.
- No text baked into the image.

## Composition for the page
- Portrait, ~4:5 (about 1024 × 1280). The left page cover-fills, so keep the key subject
  centered with ~10% breathing room on every edge; nothing essential in the corners.

## Theological / content rules
- Never depict God as a figure. Creation scenes show the world being formed, not a maker.
- Progressive disclosure: no element appears before its day. Day One light has no sun,
  moon, or stars; Days 2–3 have no celestial disks or creatures; Day Four introduces the
  sun, moon, and stars; Day Five sea life and birds; Day Six land animals and people.
- The first man and woman: depicted reverently and modestly — covered, never risqué
  (the approved Day 6 and Day 7 treatments are the reference).
- Day Seven shows a finished, peaceful world — completion, not a tired or depicted God.

## Guides (future art)
- Isaiah guides the Old Testament books; John guides the New. One guide per book; no
  rotating cast inside a chapter.
- A guide reads Scripture alongside the child and never claims to have witnessed events he
  did not. His own words are always visually distinct from the narration and from Scripture.
- Scale the guide's character with grade: light-touch for Grade 1 (Ellis), richer for the
  older grades.
